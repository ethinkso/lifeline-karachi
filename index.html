What actually happens

The donation process, step by step

Most first-time donors skip donating simply because they don't know what to expect.

Registration & ID check

You fill a short form and show a CNIC or B-form. Takes about 5 minutes.

Quick health screening

Hemoglobin, blood pressure, weight and pulse are checked on the spot — this is where eligibility is actually confirmed.

The donation itself

About 8–10 minutes, roughly 450ml collected via a single sterile, single-use needle. Most people feel a brief pinch and nothing more.

Rest & refreshment

10–15 minutes with juice and a snack before you leave. Avoid heavy exercise for the rest of the day.

Common misconceptions

Myths vs. facts

Most reasons people give for not donating don't hold up medically.

"Donating blood weakens you long-term."

Your body replaces the donated plasma within 24–48 hours and red blood cells within a few weeks. A short rest and some water afterward is all most healthy donors need.

"I have a common blood type, so my donation doesn't matter much."

Common types are needed in the highest volume precisely because they're common — hospitals go through them fastest during shortages.

"Blood donation can give you an infection."

Registered blood banks use single-use sterile needles and equipment for every donor. The risk to the donor is essentially zero at accredited centres.

"Fasting or Ramazan means I can't donate."

Donation is generally discouraged only right before iftar due to low fluids; many centres in Karachi run dedicated post-iftar donation drives during Ramazan.

"Thalassemia patients only need blood occasionally."

Many thalassemia patients need a transfusion every 2–4 weeks for life — it's one of the steadiest sources of demand on Karachi's blood banks.

"Donating is bad for the donor's own health."

Regular donors have been shown to have slightly lower average iron overload risk, and each visit includes a free basic health screening — a check on hemoglobin, blood pressure and pulse you might not otherwise get.
